# JK Plumbing Website Audit

## Overall Verdict

The current site is a safer, cleaner first draft, but it is not yet strong enough to show the client as a confident replacement for an existing direct-response plumbing site.

It avoids fake claims, which is good. The problem is that it also avoids too much tension, specificity and personality. It reads like a careful agency placeholder: polished, tidy, technically competent, but not yet like a real local plumber trying to win a call from someone with water backing up, no hot water, a leaking fixture or a renovation that needs a dependable trade.

The site has the right ingredients: licence, ABN, Campbelltown base, Sydney service area, phone number, real images and grouped services. The issue is execution. Too many sections explain the website's logic instead of selling the business. Too much copy says "clear", "practical", "properly", "next step" and "solutions" without grounding those ideas in real plumbing situations.

## 1. Why The Current Website Feels Generic

- The copy repeatedly uses safe abstractions: "clear answer", "practical next step", "properly", "without unnecessary fuss", "same standard", "different scale", "clear process". These phrases sound reasonable but not distinctive.
- The site still does not have a strong brand voice. It could be any licensed plumber in any suburb with minor name changes.
- The hero headline is descriptive, but not sharp enough: "Campbelltown plumber for repairs, renovations and maintenance done properly" is clear, but it does not create urgency or emotional relief.
- The business has a real person attached, James Khouri, but the site barely uses that to create trust. "Call James" or a direct founder-led tone should be much more present.
- The design is clean but mostly assembled from familiar Tailwind landing-page patterns: dark hero, white cards, icon grids, trust strip, form section. It is competent, not memorable.
- The site does not yet show enough evidence of local, on-the-tools work. It has real images available in `public/images`, but the gallery still uses placeholders and the real imagery is not used aggressively enough to prove authenticity.

## 2. Sections That Still Feel Like AI Slop

### Hero

The hero is improved visually with layered cards, but the copy still feels generated:

- "When plumbing interrupts the day..."
- "you want a clear answer..."
- "no shortcut fix..."
- "licensed residential, commercial and maintenance plumbing..."

These are generic sentiments. A real local plumber's site should sound more immediate:

- blocked toilet before work
- hot water gone cold
- water under the vanity
- drain backing up outside
- renovation booked and rough-in needed

The hero talks about plumbing in general instead of naming enough concrete scenarios.

### Common Problems

This section is useful in principle, but the wording still sounds like a copywriting exercise:

- "The calls usually start with a problem that needs a straight answer."
- "The first job is to understand what is happening..."
- "That keeps the first conversation useful..."

These lines are too meta. The section should sound like the customer is being understood, not like the website is explaining its content strategy.

### Services

The grouped service model is better than the old list, but the descriptions still repeat the same structure:

- urgent situation
- home situation
- project situation
- maintenance situation

The service cards do not yet answer enough practical questions: "Can you handle gas?", "Do you do rough-ins?", "Can you look at a repeat blocked drain?", "Can you work with my builder?", "Can you do commercial maintenance?"

### Why Choose

This section is the closest to generic AI copy. It uses the classic template:

- Clear communication
- Honest pricing
- No shortcuts
- Long-term solutions
- Licensed workmanship

These are acceptable proof points, but every trade website says versions of this. Without examples, they feel like claims rather than personality.

### About

The About section says James is director, but it does not make the business feel founder-led. It should be the most human section, and currently it is still a polished paragraph about what the business does.

## 3. Sections That Are Too Wordy

- The hero paragraph is too long for a first 5-second read. It tries to explain the positioning and service categories in one paragraph.
- The Common Problems intro is too explanatory. Users should be able to scan the problem cards immediately.
- The Services intro is too long and self-conscious: "Instead of a long menu of plumbing jargon..." sounds like an agency explaining a design choice.
- The Project Range section repeats the same process language already used elsewhere.
- The Contact section description is too long. The key message should be simpler: call now for urgent plumbing, use the form for planned jobs.
- The trust bar plus hero proof cards duplicate licence, ABN and local base. That is not necessarily bad, but the duplication needs to be visually intentional rather than feeling like repeated chips.

## 4. Sections That Are Not Conversion-Focused Enough

### Hero

The call button is visible, but the hero does not create enough reason to call now. The text says calling is fastest, but it lacks problem urgency. It needs stronger "if this is happening, call" language.

### Common Problems

The cards describe problems but do not end with action. Each could push toward "Call about this" or at least lead into the phone CTA.

### Services

The grouped cards are informative but passive. They list categories without showing how a user should choose what to do next.

### Gallery

The gallery currently undermines conversion because it says "Photo coming soon" six times. That signals unfinished work. For a trust-driven trades site, unfinished visual proof is worse than no gallery.

### Contact

The contact section has a strong call card, but the form may distract from the phone for urgent users. The page should bias harder toward phone calls and treat the form as secondary for non-urgent project enquiries.

## 5. Whether The Hero Has Enough Urgency

No. The hero is calm and professional, but not urgent enough for plumbing.

Plumbing buyers are often in one of these states:

- something is leaking
- water is backing up
- hot water has failed
- a toilet, tap or sink is unusable
- a builder needs a plumber booked
- a business needs maintenance handled quickly

The current hero does not make those situations vivid. It says "When plumbing interrupts the day", which is soft. It should speak more directly to the moment:

- "Blocked drain, leak or no hot water?"
- "Call a Campbelltown plumber who will talk you through the next step."
- "Repairs, maintenance, renovations and new builds across Sydney."

The current hero feels trustworthy but not enough like a response to an active problem.

## 6. Whether The Site Gives Users A Strong Reason To Call Now

Partially, but not strongly enough.

The site says the fastest path is calling. That is good. But it does not create enough friction against waiting. It avoids fake urgency, which is correct, but there are safe ways to create real urgency:

- leaks can spread
- blocked drains can worsen
- hot water faults disrupt the household
- photos and a quick call help decide the next step
- early diagnosis can prevent unnecessary work

The site should not say "same day", "24/7" or "guaranteed", but it can still say:

- "If water is backing up, call before it becomes a bigger mess."
- "If a leak has started, call before opening walls or pulling fixtures apart."
- "If you are unsure, a quick call is the fastest way to explain the issue."

Currently the reason to call now is convenience, not urgency.

## 7. Whether The Design Feels Memorable Or Just Clean

Mostly clean, not memorable.

Strengths:

- dark navy base fits the brand direction
- orange phone CTAs stand out
- layered hero visuals are stronger than the first draft
- real photos help avoid fake stock-photo energy
- mobile sticky call button is strong

Weaknesses:

- card shapes, spacing and icons are standard SaaS/agency landing-page patterns
- the visual language does not yet feel uniquely JK Plumbing
- there is no strong brand device beyond navy/orange, "JK" square and layered photos
- the section rhythm is still predictable: heading, paragraph, cards
- the real vehicle/logo imagery is not used as a first-class trust asset

The site would feel more memorable if it leaned into the actual JK Plumbing truck, equipment, job photos and Campbelltown/Sydney identity rather than abstract icon cards.

## 8. Whether The Gallery/Visual Treatment Feels Unfinished

Yes. The gallery is the weakest section.

The current gallery says:

- "Project photos, ready for real job images."
- "Photo coming soon"

That is not client-presentable. It admits the section is unfinished. Even if the user asked for placeholders originally, the site now has real images in `public/images`, so continuing to show placeholder boxes makes the site feel incomplete and lowers trust.

Better options:

1. Use the available real photos now and label them simply.
2. Remove the gallery until approved images are ready.
3. Rename it to "Recent work" and use real cropped images without pretending they are a full portfolio.

The current placeholder gallery makes the site feel like a template.

## 9. Whether The Service Sections Feel Useful Or Repetitive

The services are more useful than before because they are grouped into four categories. That is the right direction.

However, repetition remains:

- Common Problems covers blocked drains, leaks, hot water and projects.
- Services covers urgent repairs, home plumbing, renovations, commercial.
- Project Range covers small fixes, problem solving, major projects.
- Why Choose repeats clear process and long-term solutions.

These sections overlap heavily. The user gets the same message four different ways:

- call with the issue
- get a practical next step
- work is handled properly
- jobs range from small to large

The site needs fewer repeated explanations and more decisive, differentiated content:

- one section for urgent problems
- one section for service categories
- one section for real proof/work examples
- one section for founder/local trust
- one section for contact

Right now the page is longer than the amount of actual evidence supports.

## 10. Whether The Copy Sounds Like A Real Local Plumber Or A Template

It still sounds more like a polished template than a real local plumber.

Positive signs:

- Campbelltown appears in the hero, header, service area and contact guidance.
- James Khouri is named.
- Licence and ABN are visible.
- The phone number is prominent.

Template signs:

- "clear process"
- "practical next step"
- "long-term solutions"
- "same standard, different scale"
- "focused on what will actually solve the problem"
- "without unnecessary fuss"
- "business premises, fit-outs and ongoing maintenance requirements"

A real local plumber voice would be more specific and less polished:

- "Tell us what is blocked, leaking or not working."
- "If you can send a photo, do that after you call."
- "We work across Campbelltown, Macarthur, South West Sydney and wider Sydney depending on the job."
- "From tap repairs to rough-ins and fit-offs, the job gets explained before work starts."

The site should sound less like a brand manifesto and more like a competent tradesperson who knows what customers are worried about.

## 11. Whether Mobile Users Can Instantly Call

Yes. This is one of the strongest parts of the site.

The sticky mobile call button is clear:

- visible at the bottom
- orange on dark navy
- says "Tap to call"
- includes the phone number
- links to `tel:0447798126`

The mobile header also has a phone icon. This is good.

Potential issue: the sticky call button can cover footer content or lower form content if spacing is not enough. The footer has mobile bottom padding, but the contact section itself may need enough bottom breathing room if the form ends near the sticky bar.

## 12. Risky Or Unverified Claims

The site is mostly safe. It does not appear to include the high-risk claims from the old site context:

- no fake testimonials
- no fake Google reviews
- no review counts
- no star ratings
- no discounts
- no 24/7 claim
- no same-day promise
- no guarantee language
- no insurance claim
- no association or membership claim
- no years-of-experience claim

Potentially risky or worth tightening:

- "Honest pricing" is subjective. It is common, but it is still a claim. Safer wording could be "Pricing discussed before work moves ahead."
- "Long-term solutions" is also subjective. It is acceptable as positioning, but should be grounded in examples rather than sounding like a promise.
- "Emergency repairs" is listed as a service. This is probably acceptable because the user supplied it, but the site should avoid implying 24/7 or same-day availability.
- "Fastest path" is not a risky operational claim by itself, but it should be clear that this means the fastest way to start the conversation, not guaranteed attendance.
- "Licensed work for homes, businesses and project sites" is safe if the licence is accurate.

ABN, licence, phone and email are consistent because they come from `src/lib/site.ts`.

## 13. What Should Change Before Client Presentation

### Priority 1: Fix The Gallery

Do not show "Photo coming soon" to the client. Either use real available images or remove the section for now. The placeholder gallery is the clearest sign the site is unfinished.

### Priority 2: Rewrite The Hero For Urgency

The hero needs to speak to immediate plumbing pain without fake promises. It should lead with a problem/relief pairing:

- blocked drain
- leak
- no hot water
- renovation or fit-off
- call a licensed Campbelltown plumber

The current hero is safe but not compelling enough.

### Priority 3: Make James More Present

The site should use the director detail more effectively. "Director: James Khouri" currently appears like footer compliance information. The site should make the business feel founder-led and local.

Possible direction:

- "Call James at JK Plumbing Solutions"
- "Based in Campbelltown and working across Sydney"
- "You will get the job explained before work moves ahead"

Only use this if it matches how the business actually operates.

### Priority 4: Replace Abstract Claims With Concrete Examples

Current phrasing:

- clear process
- practical next step
- long-term solutions
- no shortcuts

Better phrasing:

- "Blocked drain? We look at what is causing the blockage before recommending the fix."
- "Renovation booked? We can handle rough-ins and fit-offs around the build schedule."
- "Hot water failing? Call through the issue and system type."

### Priority 5: Reduce Repetition

The page currently has too many sections saying similar things. Combine or sharpen:

- Common Problems should be more urgent and direct.
- Services should be the main categorisation section.
- Project Range should either become a stronger visual proof/process section or be removed.
- Why Choose should use concrete standards instead of generic values.

### Priority 6: Use Real Visual Proof More Aggressively

The available image set should be used more:

- company vehicle image for local legitimacy
- drain camera/equipment image for blocked drains
- bathroom/kitchen images for renovation quality
- tap/leak image for maintenance and repairs

The visuals should feel like "this is their actual work/equipment", not decorative placeholders.

### Priority 7: Make Contact More Phone-Led

The contact section is good, but it should make phone the obvious primary path:

- "For anything leaking, blocked or not working, call first."
- "For renovation/new build enquiries, send details through the form."

The form should not compete equally with the phone for urgent issues.

### Priority 8: Keep The Safer Claim Standard

Do not import the old site's aggressive claims unless the client confirms them in writing:

- discounts
- warranties or guarantees
- insurance
- review counts
- star ratings
- 7-day availability
- same-day attendance
- association memberships
- years in business

The new site can be more urgent without becoming legally or reputationally risky.

## Brutal Summary

The current site is presentable as an internal draft, not as a client-ready replacement.

It is clean, safe and technically competent, but it still sounds too much like a cautious AI-written trade website. It needs fewer abstract promises, more real-world plumbing situations, stronger founder/local presence, a fixed gallery, and a hero that makes a stressed visitor feel like calling is the obvious next move.

The biggest weakness is not design quality. It is lack of proof and personality. The site should feel like JK Plumbing Solutions specifically, not "licensed plumber template with navy/orange styling."
